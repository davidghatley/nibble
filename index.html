01 · The problem

Your agent asks a big model lots of small questions, like which template to start from or which art style fits. Each one waits on a written reply that never says how sure the model was.

02 · What you get

Benefits

2.1Pick

A pick, with no essay

nibble scores every option you give it and hands back a pick, with no paragraph to wait for.

Example A frog who fishes by lantern light. Which template?
top-down 81% · cozy 12% · arena 7% (example numbers)
2.2Confidence

Know when to double-check

Every pick comes with a calibrated confidence score, so you decide which ones go to a person or a bigger model.

ExampleSet a rule like under 70% sure, ask the big model. Easy picks stay fast, and only the unsure ones cost more.
2.3Train

Train a decision model on your agent logs

Turn choices logged in past runs into a small model of your own that runs on a home graphics card or a CPU.

ExampleTrain on your logged game builds, then run the result on one 12 GB graphics card.
03 · How it works

How it works

  1. Ask your question.Send the situation and your options as JSON (structured text programs read).
  2. Read the pick.You get the pick, the odds for every option, and how long it took.
  3. Train your own.Point the trainer at decisions logged from past runs, and get a smaller decider tuned to your task.

See it work

05 · Who it's for

Who it's for

Yes For you if

  • Your agents make the same kinds of choice again and again.
  • You pay a big model to answer multiple-choice questions.
  • You want those choices to run on your own hardware.

No Not for you if

  • You need writing, chatting or summarising. nibble only picks from the options you give it.
  • Your questions are one-offs and a few seconds don't matter.
  • You need a finished tool today.

07 · FAQ

FAQ

Why not just ask GPT or Claude?

You can, and that's the bar. nibble earns its place only if it is faster and at least as good on prompts it never trained on. That test is the first demo. It isn't an LLM router either: a router picks which model answers, and nibble is the model that answers.

What is Kev?

Kev is Jared Palmer's open family of small decision models (Apache-2.0), an open alternative to the closed Jev. nibble installs Kev as a dependency, credits it, and adds game questions, the big-model test and the trainer.

How fast is it?

We haven't measured nibble yet. The only published figure is Kev's, measured by Kev's team. Kev's README reports Kev-4B answering six questions about a short text in 18.1 ms of model time on an H100, a data-centre GPU. nibble targets a 12 GB home graphics card or a CPU, which will be slower. Its first demo times every pick.

Can I trust the "how sure" number?

It is calibrated, so a pick marked 80% sure should be right about 8 times in 10. It can drift on a new task, so nibble re-checks it on answers kept aside for testing.

Is it only for games?

For now. playdough needs games first, so games are all we're testing.

Can I try it today?

No. Nothing has shipped. Read the plan, and watch it get built.
